Drop rebound at low Weber number

We study drop rebound on non-wetting substrates at low Weber number
via experiment, direct numerical simulation, and a reduced-order
model. Submillimeter drops impact glass slides coated with a thin
viscous film that enables rebound without contact-line formation. We
find that at low Weber number, the coefficient of restitution depends
more weakly on Weber number than in the high-Weber regime studied
previously; higher-viscosity or larger drops interact with the
substrate for longer, have lower coefficients of restitution, and
stop bouncing sooner.

The kinematic match model formulation expands the drop surface
deformation and the contact pressure in axisymmetric spherical
harmonic modes. A circular contact region forms where the substrate
exerts pressure on the drop, with the contact boundary determined
implicitly by requiring the drop surface be tangent to the substrate
at that boundary.
